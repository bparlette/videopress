# Shorts Rubric — the 13 rules (MrBeast audit) + Cherie-line amendments

Score every Short against this list before it ships. Pass / partial / fail
per rule, one line of evidence each. Distilled Nov 2026 from an audit of
MrBeast's Shorts against the two questions Beast Industries hires on —
**"What worked?"** and **"What would you change?"** — then amended after
the same rubric was run on our own three lines (AiDiaries, ReadDash,
Hospital Escape game shorts). Source audits:
`research_notes/mrbeast-shorts-audit/` and
`research_notes/cherie-shorts-audit/` in the workspace.

## The 13 rules

- [ ] **1. Frame zero is a consequence, not a premise.** The first frame is
  something already happening or already gone wrong. Never open on
  stillness, a title card, or an empty room/road.
- [ ] **2. The question is visible without sound, language, or context.**
  A muted stranger scrolling at speed can state the video's question from
  the picture alone.
- [ ] **3. Show the progress bar — and leave one slot a mystery.** The
  viewer can always see how far along the video is and what remains.
- [ ] **4. Payoff every ~10 seconds; build in rounds.** Small completed
  payoffs on a clock, structured as rounds, not one payoff at the end.
- [ ] **5. Escalate by register, and announce it.** Weird → dangerous →
  cute → absurd (or the line's equivalent ladder). Say the escalation out
  loud; don't just let it happen.
- [ ] **6. The finale breaks the format's own rule.** The last beat
  violates the pattern the video itself established.
- [ ] **7. Weld the CTA to the climax as a bet on the outcome.** Never end
  on an end card, a teaser for the next video, or a bare subscribe ask.
- [ ] **8. Stakes must have a fate on screen.** Anything introduced as
  being at risk gets resolved in the picture — including props.
- [ ] **9. Kill any stretch that could run as an ad unchanged** — or give
  it a clock and a consequence.
- [ ] **10. Loops serve retention, not reach.** The loop is a bonus;
  spend the effort on the first second, not the seam.
- [ ] **11. High like rate + low views = hook problem, not content
  problem.** Diagnose with the ratio before reshooting the content.
- [ ] **12. Borrowed fame must be legible unlabeled.** If a celebrity,
  brand, or borrowed clip needs a caption to land, it isn't landing.
- [ ] **13. One joke caps at ~30 seconds** unless each repetition changes
  the stakes. Test: could the back third be shuffled or cut unnoticed?
  Then it's dead time.

## Amendments for our lines (Nov 24, 2026)

- [ ] **A1. Format is rule zero.** A landscape video is not a Short at any
  length; the Shorts feed never sees it. Check aspect before hooks.
- [ ] **A2. For pre-reader audiences, the picture is the promise**
  (extends 8). Illustrations must not promise what the words won't
  deliver, and a question-story's answer stays out of the picture until
  the reveal sentence. (ReadDash)
- [ ] **A3. Silence is a frame-zero property too** (extends 1). In a
  muted-autoplay feed, audio can't carry a hook — and a wholly silent
  track on an action property reads as broken, not calm. Measure the
  track; don't assume it.
- [ ] **A4. The game's own HUD counts as the progress bar** (clarifies 3
  for game shorts). Score counters and health bars already in frame
  satisfy the rule. Prefer beats where they're visible; never crop them
  out.
- [ ] **A5. Literacy cadence is per-sentence** (clarifies 4 and 7). The
  completed word set is the round payoff; the repeat pause sets the
  clock. CTAs and borrowed fame don't apply to ages 3–5 — the
  parent-facing ask lives in the title and description. Hard rules that
  outrank this rubric: words appear whole, never spelled letter by
  letter; no sentence-end ding. (ReadDash)
- [ ] **A6. Rule 11 has a floor.** Below ~100 views, like-rate splits are
  noise. Audit the artifact and the distribution (format, title,
  duplicate uploads) separately.

## Line-specific standing rules (outrank the rubric)

- **Game shorts:** no words or text on screen ever (the game's own HUD
  and dialogue are part of the picture); real game audio only, untouched;
  variants of a moment must be completely different angles, never the
  same footage re-cut. Template short: open mid-action, HUD visible, end
  on a line or a resolution, ≤25 s.
- **ReadDash:** ages 3–5 + parents. Judge hooks and stakes
  age-appropriately — no money stakes, no manufactured peril. A named
  animal doing something, or a question the child wants answered, is the
  correct hook scale.
- **AiDiaries:** internal/brand rules in the channel's own docs apply;
  nothing about the channel's production is published outside it.
